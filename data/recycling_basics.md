# Recycling Basics in India

## What is Recycling?

Recycling is the process of converting waste materials into new products. This helps reduce the consumption of fresh raw materials, energy usage, air pollution, and water pollution.

## Why Recycling Matters in India

India generates over **62 million tonnes** of waste annually, with only about **60% of plastic waste** being recycled. Proper recycling can:

- Reduce landfill burden
- Conserve natural resources
- Create employment opportunities
- Reduce greenhouse gas emissions
- Save energy

## Waste Segregation at Source

### Three-Bin System

**Green Bin (Wet Waste)**

- Food waste and leftovers
- Vegetable and fruit peels
- Garden waste and leaves
- Tea bags and coffee grounds
- Eggshells

**Blue Bin (Dry Recyclable Waste)**

- Paper, cardboard, and newspapers
- Plastic bottles and containers
- Glass bottles and jars
- Metal cans and containers
- Tetra packs

**Red/Black Bin (Hazardous/Reject Waste)**

- Sanitary napkins and diapers
- Batteries
- Medicine strips and syringes
- Broken glass and ceramics
- LED bulbs and tube lights

## Plastic Recycling Guide

### Plastic Types and Recyclability

**Type 1 (PET) - Highly Recyclable**

- Water bottles, soft drink bottles
- Can be recycled into: Clothing, carpets, new bottles

**Type 2 (HDPE) - Highly Recyclable**

- Milk jugs, detergent bottles, shampoo bottles
- Can be recycled into: Plastic lumber, pipes, bottles

**Type 3 (PVC) - Limited Recyclability**

- Pipes, flooring, vinyl siding
- Generally not accepted in curbside recycling

**Type 4 (LDPE) - Sometimes Recyclable**

- Shopping bags, garbage bags
- Check with local recyclers

**Type 5 (PP) - Increasingly Recyclable**

- Yogurt containers, bottle caps
- Can be recycled into: Brooms, brushes, battery cables

**Type 6 (PS) - Rarely Recyclable**

- Styrofoam cups, packing peanuts
- Not typically accepted in recycling programs

**Type 7 (Other) - Generally Not Recyclable**

- Mixed plastics, CDs, DVDs
- Usually not recyclable through standard programs

## E-Waste Recycling

E-waste includes:

- Old mobile phones and chargers
- Computers and laptops
- TVs and monitors
- Printers and scanners
- Batteries
- LED bulbs

**How to Dispose E-Waste Properly:**

1. Contact authorized e-waste collection centers
2. Use manufacturer take-back programs
3. Donate working electronics to NGOs
4. Never throw e-waste in regular bins

**Authorized E-Waste Recyclers:**

- Contact your State Pollution Control Board
- Check with electronics retailers
- Look for certified e-waste collection drives

## Paper Recycling

**What Can Be Recycled:**

- Newspapers and magazines
- Office paper
- Cardboard boxes
- Paper bags
- Envelopes (without plastic windows)

**What Cannot Be Recycled:**

- Paper with food stains
- Wax-coated paper
- Carbon paper
- Laminated paper
- Tissue paper and napkins

## Glass and Metal Recycling

**Glass:**

- Clean bottles and jars are 100% recyclable
- Remove metal caps and lids
- Rinse containers before recycling
- Broken glass should be wrapped separately

**Metal:**

- Aluminum cans and foil
- Steel cans and containers
- Metal caps and lids
- Clean, dry metal is easier to recycle

## Composting at Home

Convert wet waste into nutrient-rich compost:

**Simple Composting Steps:**

1. Use a composting bin or pit
2. Layer green waste (food scraps) and brown waste (dried leaves)
3. Keep the pile moist but not wet
4. Turn the pile every few weeks
5. Compost is ready in 2-3 months

**Benefits:**

- Reduces waste going to landfills
- Creates free fertilizer for plants
- Reduces methane emissions
- Improves soil health

## Recycling Guidelines by State

Many Indian states have specific recycling regulations:

**Maharashtra:**

- Mandatory waste segregation at source
- Door-to-door collection systems
- User fees for waste management

**Karnataka:**

- Strict enforcement of segregation
- No plastic bags below 50 microns
- Composting mandated for large generators

**Delhi:**

- Color-coded bin system
- E-waste collection drives
- Construction waste recycling units

## Reduce, Reuse, Recycle

Before recycling, consider:

**Reduce:**

- Buy products with minimal packaging
- Avoid single-use plastics
- Choose reusable items

**Reuse:**

- Use cloth bags instead of plastic
- Refill water bottles
- Donate unwanted items
- Repurpose containers

**Recycle:**

- Segregate at source
- Clean recyclables before disposing
- Support circular economy

## Economic Benefits

Recycling creates jobs:

- Collection and sorting: 6 jobs per 10,000 tonnes
- Recycling: 30-40 jobs per 10,000 tonnes
- Compared to landfilling: Only 1 job per 10,000 tonnes

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Wish-cycling**: Don't recycle contaminated items
2. **Dirty recyclables**: Rinse containers first
3. **Plastic bags in recycling**: These jam machinery
4. **Mixed waste**: Always segregate properly
5. **Ignoring hazardous waste**: Follow proper disposal methods
